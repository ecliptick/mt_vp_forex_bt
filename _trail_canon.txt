5e. THE GEOMETRY  (how wide can the trail be?)
==============================================================================
A trail fires at bar b iff the width is SMALL ENOUGH that the
retrace reaches it. So a WIDER trail fires LESS:
    chandelier (long): fires iff  k <= (peak - low)/ATR_1m
    giveback  (long): fires iff  f <= (peak - low)/MFE

    K_tp = the width at which the trail CUTS the TP
          -> the TP is preserved iff  k > K_tp
    K_sl = the width at which the trail RESCUES the loser

9. WHY THE ENGINE SWEEP SAID THE TRAIL HURTS
==============================================================================
The replay and the engine disagree because they measure
different things. The replay isolates the TRAIL'S OWN arithmetic
on a FIXED book. The engine lets the trail change the BOOK:

  * a trail-closed leg frees a same-direction slot, so a LATER
    signal is admitted that would not have been;
  * a trail-closed leg is no longer in the book, so it cannot
    be netted by contra-close;
  * the engine's 'combined' mode takes the TIGHTER of the two
    levels, so a tight chandelier dominates the giveback and
    the giveback leg of the mode is inert.

The engine's own numbers for the SAME config (from the engine run
above, {'trail_mode': 'combined', 'trail_atr_mult': 2.0, 'trail_activation_mult': 1.0, 'trail_giveback_frac': 0.5}):
    legs 131 (vs 131 trail-off)  gross $-9.01  fees $61.62  net $-70.63
    trail-stopped legs: 107

A trail that fires on FEW legs and keeps the rest's exits intact
is a RISK knob (it truncates the loss tail). A trail that fires
on MANY legs and replaces the TP is a GEOMETRY change (and here
it loses, because the TP is worth more than the trail captures).
